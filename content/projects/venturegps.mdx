<CaseStudySection id="executive-overview" title="Executive Overview">

VentureGPS is a startup intelligence platform built to help founders and
investors make better long-term decisions using structured startup
analysis, rather than gut feel or scattered spreadsheets. It combines a
deterministic scoring methodology with AI-assisted research, so the number
an investor sees is reproducible and defensible — not just something a
language model generated on request.

This case study covers the platform's architecture, the engineering
decisions behind its scoring system and ingestion pipeline, and how
deterministic business logic is kept separate from AI-assisted analysis.

</CaseStudySection>

<CaseStudySection id="system-overview" title="System Overview">

At the center of VentureGPS is the **Startup Profile** — a living history
of a company that accumulates structured information over time, rather
than a one-off snapshot. Profiles feed a set of shared intelligence
features used across the platform: rankings, search, scoring,
benchmarking, and trend analysis.

The product is organized around two audiences with different workflows:

- **Founders** — pitch-deck review, fundraising readiness, coaching,
  progress tracking, benchmarking, data-room review, and
  investor-readiness workflows.
- **Investors** — portfolio tracking, discovery filters, watchlists,
  alerts, memo export, thesis tagging, and saved views.

Not every one of these workflows is at the same level of maturity; they
describe the product's intended shape rather than a claim that each is
fully built out.

### Scoring methodology

Every startup is scored across six weighted pillars, producing an overall
score from 0–100 alongside a confidence rating.

| Pillar | Weight |
| --- | --- |
| Market | 20% |
| Team | 20% |
| Product | 20% |
| Execution | 15% |
| Traction | 15% |
| Financial Health | 10% |

Confidence is reported as **Low**, **Medium**, or **High**, and scoring is
stage-aware — a pre-seed company and a Series B+ company aren't held to
the same evidentiary bar for, say, traction or financial health. Supported
stages are Pre-seed, Seed, Series A, and Series B+.

</CaseStudySection>

<CaseStudySection id="architecture" title="Architecture">

VentureGPS is a full-stack product: a Next.js frontend in front of a
backend intelligence layer responsible for startup scoring, readiness
summaries, analysis, search, and analytics. A distinct ingestion path
accepts a startup's website URL and analyzes it through a dedicated
`/analyze-website` endpoint.

Because that ingestion path accepts a URL supplied by a user and fetches
it server-side, it's treated as a security-sensitive boundary rather than
a convenience feature — see [Engineering Decisions](#engineering-decisions).

</CaseStudySection>

<CaseStudySection id="engineering-decisions" title="Engineering Decisions">

<CaseStudyCallout
  kind="decision"
  title="Treat website ingestion as a security boundary, not a convenience feature"
>

<Field label="Problem">

Letting a user submit an arbitrary website URL for analysis means the
server ends up fetching a URL it doesn't control. Without safeguards,
that request path can be abused to reach internal services, cloud
metadata endpoints, or other network resources that were never meant to
be reachable from the outside.

</Field>

<Field label="Alternatives">

Fetch whatever URL the user submits and rely on downstream code to behave
correctly; or route ingestion through a third-party proxy or screenshot
service instead of fetching server-side at all.

</Field>

<Field label="Why">

Handling this in-house — with explicit SSRF protections around the
`/analyze-website` endpoint and dedicated security tests — keeps full
control over what the ingestion path is and isn't allowed to reach,
without taking on a dependency (and its cost) for something the
application can own directly.

</Field>

<Field label="Tradeoffs">

Owning SSRF protection means owning its correctness and keeping it
current as new bypass techniques surface, instead of delegating that
responsibility to a vendor.

</Field>

</CaseStudyCallout>

<CaseStudyCallout
  kind="decision"
  title="Keep the scoring engine deterministic and separate from AI-assisted analysis"
>

<Field label="Problem">

Investors and founders need a score they can trust, compare across
companies, and reproduce — not a number that might change if the same
startup were re-evaluated by a language model tomorrow.

</Field>

<Field label="Alternatives">

Score companies primarily through LLM prompting, or blend AI output
directly into the numeric score.

</Field>

<Field label="Why">

A fixed, weighted, six-pillar formula is explainable and stable: an
investor can see exactly why a company scored the way it did, and the
same inputs always produce the same score. AI is used for research and
interpretation, not to move the number.

</Field>

<Field label="Tradeoffs">

Qualitative signal an AI model might notice has to stay advisory — in the
analysis and narrative around a company — rather than directly changing
its score.

</Field>

</CaseStudyCallout>

<CaseStudySection id="production-engineering" title="Production Engineering">

Known production-engineering evidence for VentureGPS centers on the
ingestion boundary: the `/analyze-website` endpoint that accepts a
startup's website URL is SSRF-hardened and backed by dedicated security
tests, rather than left as an open fetch. The backend intelligence layer —
scoring, readiness summaries, analysis, search, and analytics — sits
behind the Next.js frontend as a distinct service boundary.

Further production-engineering detail, such as deployment and
observability specifics, will be added here as it's documented.

</CaseStudySection>

<CaseStudySection id="ai-engineering" title="AI Engineering">

VentureGPS treats AI as probabilistic infrastructure, not an authority
over the platform's core numbers. The scoring pillars, weights, and
stage-aware logic are deterministic business rules — AI is used for
research and interpretation *around* a company, not to compute or
override its score.

That distinction matters for anyone evaluating the platform: an investor
who disagrees with an AI-generated summary can still trust the score
underneath it, because the score isn't something the AI produced.

</CaseStudySection>

<CaseStudySection id="scaling-evolution" title="Scaling & Evolution">

VentureGPS's split between a deterministic scoring core and an
AI-assisted research and interpretation layer is also a scaling decision:
the two can evolve independently. The scoring methodology can be revised —
new pillars, adjusted weights, additional stages — without touching how
AI-assisted research is generated, and vice versa.

No production-scale metrics are published here, because none have been
verified for this portfolio.
